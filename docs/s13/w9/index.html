Where the playoff line falls

Averaging the 6 seed's wins with the 7 seed's across a billion seasons: mean 13.996, median 14.0, mode 14.0 — 14 wins is the bubble in 26.1% of seasons, and 13 through 15 covers 90%.

Practical read: 15 wins should get you in, 14 is a coin flip, 13 probably isn't enough.

Why the top seeds matter

  • Seeds 1 and 2 get a bye through week 15.
  • Seed 1 also chooses its week-16 opponent from the week-15 winners.
  • Seed 3 chooses its week-15 opponent from seeds 4, 5 and 6.
  • Seeds 4, 5 and 6 are all in the playoffs — there is simply nothing separating them. No bye, no choice, you play whoever is left. That's why they're grouped together everywhere below.

This order is not Yahoo's order

Yahoo breaks ties on points. We don't — our rule is most recent weekly records counting backwards. It already matters: XXI (8-8) sits ahead of The Nightman Re-Cometh (8-8) here on a week-9 2-0 against an 0-2, even though Nightman has scored 2.91 more points on the season. Yahoo shows that pair the other way round.

Side money

The season-long prizes

All twelve teams, ranked as the prize would be awarded today with the league tiebreakers applied.

Assuming no ties. Every number here assumes no tied matchups and no tied medians — scores always separate. Ties in the standings are handled in full, using the league rule: most ties, then most recent weekly records counting backwards from week 14, then points. That points tiebreak can never actually be reached, because every pair of teams meets at least once during the season and in that week their records cannot be equal.

Where the numbers come from. Each simulated week is drawn as a random ordering of all twelve teams — equal weight, nobody rated better than anybody else. Everything else — the clinching scenarios, the help needed, and the win ranges — is exact, solved against every schedule-feasible way weeks 9 through 14 could finish. Records and scores verified against Yahoo's own standings.
